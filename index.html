What is ocean pollution?

Ocean pollution is when harmful waste and substances enter the marine environment and negatively affect the ocean, coastal areas and marine life. In Aotearoa New Zealand, pollution can come from both land and sea. Plastic waste is a major problem because rubbish on land can be carried through streams and rivers before reaching the ocean. The Ministry for the Environment reported that in 2023, 67% of litter recorded in New Zealand beach surveys was plastic, showing how common plastic pollution is around our coast. Pollution can also include sediment, chemicals and wastewater, which can damage marine habitats and affect the animals that depend on them.

The problems of Microplastics

Microplastics are another major concern because they are extremely small and difficult to remove once they enter the environment . New Zealand research found microplastics in sediments in Queen Charlotte Sound/Tōtaranui, including particles buried up to 50 centimetres below the seabed. Scientists have also found microplastics in New Zealand fish, showing that these tiny pieces of plastic can enter marine food chains. This means plastic pollution does not simply remain as visible rubbish on beaches; it can break down, spread through marine environments and affect organisms throughout the food web.
