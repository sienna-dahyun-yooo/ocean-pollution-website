Why should we prevent ocean pollution?

Preventing ocean pollution is important because it can seriously harm marine animals. Animals can mistake plastic for food or become affected by rubbish in the ocean. The Ministry for the Environment reports that marine litter, including plastics and microplastics, puts marine habitats at risk. The Department of Conservation has also identified plastic pollution as one of the threats faced by leatherback turtles travelling through New Zealand waters. Seabirds such as northern royal albatrosses/toroa are also affected by plastic pollution. When marine animals eat plastic or become trapped in rubbish, they can become seriously injured or die, so reducing pollution can directly help protect marine life.

It is also important to prevent pollution because it becomes much harder to remove once it enters the ocean. Plastic can travel through rivers, break into microplastics and become buried in marine sediments. New Zealand scientists have found microplastics deep beneath the seabed, showing that pollution can remain in marine environments long after it enters the water. Cleaning beaches can remove some larger rubbish, but it cannot easily remove tiny plastic particles that have already spread through sediments and food chains. Preventing pollution before it reaches the ocean is therefore much more effective than trying to clean it up afterwards.
